# Gate A · fourteen runs, two corpora, and nothing that replicates

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Corpus:** [`corpus/gate-a.json`](../corpus/gate-a.json), 30 repositories, pinned
**Binary:** built with Go 1.25, so the compiled-in type checker accepts the whole corpus

```sh
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --ablate --cases 5                      # 2
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --ablate --cases 5 --collapse max       # 1
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --ablate --cases 5 --target corrected   # 3
lectio backtest --coupling */                                                       # 4
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --ablate --cases 5 --target symbols     # 5
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --ablate --cases 5 \
    --target corrected-symbols                                                      # 6
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a-holdout.json --candidates --cases 5          # 8
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --ablate --cases 12                     # 9
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a-holdout.json --candidates --cases 5 \
    --sweep-ratio                                                                   # 10
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --target symbols --cases 5 \
    --sweep-ratio                                                                   # 11
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --target symbols --cases 12             # 12
lectio backtest --replay holdout.json                                              # 13
lectio backtest --corpus corpus/gate-a.json --candidates --cases 12 --sweep-ratio  # 14
```

Run 7 is run 5's command again after the matched-pair column was added: same
corpus, same case set `0d1e81666c55`, same precision figures to the decimal.
Only the question is new.

Run 8 is the first run against a second corpus. Everything above it is one
sample; run 8 is the test of whether any of it generalizes.

Runs 9 through 11 add error bars and sweep the one constant the matched measure
rests on. They are the runs that decide how much of everything above survives,
and the answer is: the direction, not the magnitudes.

Run 13 is run 10's report replayed through the analysis that landed after it —
no corpus touched. Run 14 takes its one lead back to the first corpus, and
closes it.

## Verdict: FAIL, and the failure is real

The first run at scale produced a FAIL that could be read two ways: the ranking
is not good enough, or the metric rewards size and a size baseline wins by
construction. Both fit the number. Three runs later the fork is closed.

| Run | Change | lectio | largest files | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the original, `--collapse max` | 43.2% | 48.1% | −4.9 |
| 2 | harness size bias removed | 41.9% | 48.1% | −6.2 |
| 3 | the spec's tiebreaker target | 28.5% | 35.5% | −7.0 |

Removing the bias made lectio *worse*. Controlling for size did not exonerate
it. Changing the target variable made it worse again. The metric was not the
problem.

A fourth run tested the differentiator directly rather than through
precision@10, on a far larger sample than the gate uses: **lift 1.02 across
2,311 newcomer corrective commits.** Hidden coupling has no relationship to
where newcomers go wrong.

Runs 5 and 6 removed the file-path prior entirely by grading declarations
instead of files — the one remaining explanation under which the ranking could
be better than it measured. Lectio beats all four baselines there and is beaten
about two to one by a control that simply ranks the longest declarations:

| Run | Target | lectio | best baseline | largest symbols |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | symbols | 10.5% | 8.3% | **22.8%** |
| 6 | corrected symbols | 7.7% | 1.9% | **17.1%** |

Size did not come from grading file paths. It followed the measure down to
symbols and won there too.

A seventh run removed size by construction rather than by stratification, and
it is the one that settles the question at declaration granularity. Every
strategy scores chance:

| Strategy | precision@10 | size-matched |
| --- | --- | --- |
| largest symbols *(control)* | 22.8% | 52.0% |
| lectio | 10.5% | 49.5% |
| most churned, 12mo | 8.3% | 49.9% |
| largest files | 6.9% | 48.4% |
| size-proportional draw | 6.0% | 51.2% |
| most recently modified | 4.7% | 49.2% |

**Nothing carries information about which declaration a newcomer will touch,
once the two candidates are the same size.**

At file granularity the same instrument is not flat: the three history-derived
baselines sit above chance and both size-derived strategies sit on it. An
eighth run tested that split, and four other named hypotheses, against a second
corpus of thirty repositories that produced none of the numbers above.

| Finding | gate-a | holdout | Survived? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| history beats size on matched pairs | yes | yes | **yes** |
| removing orphaning is worth +3 pp | +3.0 | −0.1 | no |
| churn is the best single signal | 55.8% | 56.7% | beaten by recency |
| recency is the best predictor | 53.5% | **59.2%** | **yes** |

Runs 9 and 10 later showed the +3.0 was +1.9 with overlapping intervals on the
corpus that produced it, and that the whole table shifts by about two points
once the pairing bound is tightened from 1.25x to 1.10x.

One finding replicated as a *direction* — history strategies above size
strategies, on both corpora and at every pairing bound. Nothing has replicated
as a magnitude or as a significance. Every candidate that looked like an effect
has been the largest number in a table of ten, and each one has come back a
coin on the other corpus:

| Claimed | Where it was found | On the other corpus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| removing orphaning is worth +3.0 pp | gate-a | −0.1 pp |
| churn-only wins 12 of 15 repositories | holdout, p = 0.035 | 9 of 15, p = 0.607 |
| four strategies clear chance at 1.10x | holdout | none clear |
| history ranks above size | both | **both** |

Two further runs then put error bars on all of it. Run 9 re-ran the first
corpus with intervals and a per-repository sign test; run 10 swept the pairing
ratio the whole measure rests on. Between them:

- The orphaning result was **+1.9 points with overlapping intervals** on the
  corpus that produced it, not +3.0. It was never an effect.
- The margin is **roughly ±5 points** at file granularity, not the ±2 that had
  been written down and used to read every table.
- At **1.10x**, the tightest pairing with usable coverage, four strategies
  clear chance and all four are history-derived. Both size strategies sit on
  it. The split is sharper there than at 1.25x.
- Nothing clears chance **repository by repository**. The best sign test across
  nine runs is 11 of 15 at p = 0.12.

Run 11 then swept the bound at declaration granularity, where the file-level
result had implied it might matter just as much. It does not: declarations are
dense in size space, so tightening from 2.00x to 1.00x costs 18% of the pairs
there against 81% at file level, and no row moves more than a point. That makes
the exact-match column the strongest measurement in this document — 2,496 pairs
between declarations of *identical* length, across 24 repositories, with every
strategy between 47.6% and 51.4% and none of them clear of chance.

**So the two granularities do not deserve equal confidence.** The file-level
numbers are sensitive to a constant nobody derived and to a case set that moves
between runs. The declaration-level numbers are sensitive to neither: four
runs, one fingerprint, and a ladder that changes nothing.

Run 12 then doubled the declaration sample to 3,823 pairs from 115 cases across
25 repositories. Not one interval excludes chance and not one sign test falls
below p = 0.42. That is the strongest form of the result this document reaches,
and it is the one to quote.

## Run 1 · the first result was partly the harness's fault

The backtest ranks symbols and scores files, and the rule bridging the two was
max — applied implicitly, by walking the score-sorted symbol list and taking
each file at its first appearance. That reads as neutral and is not: a file
with sixty symbols gets sixty chances to place high, a file with three gets
three.

Measured across five corpus repositories, it put lectio's top ten files at 3.5×
the repository's median symbol count, overlapping the largest-files baseline
6/10. The harness was scoring a partial reimplementation of the baseline lectio
lost to.

The rule is now explicit, defaults to mean, and is reported with every result.
Re-running with `--collapse max` reproduces 43.2% to the decimal, so the
original number was sound — it was measuring something slightly different from
what it claimed.

**The fix cost lectio 1.3 points.** That direction is the finding. If max had
been inflating the *baseline*, removing it would have helped; instead it was
inflating lectio, which had been quietly borrowing the winning strategy.

## Run 2 · size-controlled, and the ranking still adds nothing

```
  strategy                      prec@10     recall        MRR   median
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  lectio                          41.9%      41.2%      0.702    40.0%
  largest files                   48.1%      45.7%      0.751    50.0%
  size-proportional draw          44.1%      42.8%      0.637    50.0%
  most churned, 12mo              41.5%      36.1%      0.704    40.0%
  most distinct authors           41.1%      35.8%      0.669    40.0%
  most recently modified          40.2%      35.8%      0.564    40.0%
```

**A random draw weighted by file size scores 44.1%** — above the seven-signal
ranking. It is a control rather than a baseline and does not decide the gate,
but it is the single most informative row in the table. Whatever the ranking is
computing, a coin flip biased by file length does it better.

The stratified table splits the same cases by candidate file size:

```
  strategy                   Q1 smallest          Q2          Q3  Q4 largest
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  lectio                           24.5%       27.3%       33.6%       51.7%
  largest files                    30.0%       27.5%       33.9%       53.1%
  most churned, 12mo               20.3%       20.9%       32.3%       50.1%
  most distinct authors            18.9%       18.9%       32.6%       49.9%
  most recently modified           20.0%       19.5%       31.3%       50.1%
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  size spread within band            23x          2x          2x          7x
```

Lectio wins no band. The Simpson's-paradox pattern — behind overall, ahead
everywhere — would have shown the metric rewarding size, and it is absent. The
reading rules were committed before the run; this is the branch they landed on,
not one chosen afterwards.

**The spread row is why the conclusion is narrower than "the ranking is bad".**
Quartiles by file count do not equalize size within a band: Q1 spans 23× and Q4
spans 7×, so a size strategy still holds real size information inside them, and
winning there is weak evidence about choices. Only Q2 and Q3 are tight, at 2×
each — and there the gap is **0.2 and 0.3 points**.

So where size is genuinely held constant, lectio and largest-files are the same
strategy. The ranking is not making worse choices. It is making the same ones,
for a great deal more computation.

## Run 3 · the tiebreaker fails too, and by more

The spec anticipated this and named a way out:

> weight toward files where a newcomer's early commits got reverted or fixed

That is a different target variable rather than a different weighting: score
against where newcomers *went wrong*, not where they went. It is also the
target hidden coupling should be strongest on, since touched-files is precisely
the measure a coupling signal should be weakest on.

```
Gate A · 40 cases scored, 74 discarded
  65 had too little ground truth on the corrected target to score

  strategy                      prec@10     recall        MRR   median
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  lectio                          28.5%      35.2%      0.640    30.0%
  largest files                   35.5%      45.9%      0.681    35.0%
  size-proportional draw          32.7%      43.3%      0.538    30.0%
  most churned, 12mo              31.5%      35.7%      0.623    30.0%
  most distinct authors           29.8%      34.3%      0.615    29.3%
  most recently modified          28.2%      34.1%      0.514    29.3%
```

Lectio now loses to **three** baselines instead of one, and largest-files leads
every size band including the tight ones — Q3 by 11 points. The size-weighted
coin flip beats it here too.

Two caveats, neither of which rescues the result. Only 49 of 114 cases carry
two or more corrected files, so this runs on 40 cases after degraded revisions
are discarded; and `MinCorrectedFiles` is deliberately 2 rather than 3, because
requiring more would select the contributors who broke the most things — a
different population, not a smaller sample. The direction is consistent enough
across bands that sample size is not what is producing it.

## What each signal is worth

| Signal | touched (run 2) | corrected (run 3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Centrality | **+3.8 pp** | −0.5 pp |
| Churn | +2.4 pp | +1.5 pp |
| Hidden coupling | −0.8 pp | −0.5 pp |
| Fix density | −0.1 pp | 0.0 pp |
| Orphaning | +0.5 pp | +0.8 pp |
| AI density | 0.0 pp | 0.0 pp |

Read the columns together and only one signal survives both: churn — the
cheapest of the seven, and the one the spec warns against becoming a proxy for.

Centrality was the ranking's best signal on touched-files and turns slightly
negative on corrected-files. That is coherent rather than contradictory: highly
central code is well-trodden and well-tested, so a newcomer is *less* likely to
break it. It is also the opposite of what the reading path is built to assume.

**Hidden coupling is negative on both targets.** The deltas are too small to
claim it is harmful, but it was given the target it should perform best on and
did not. Run 4 tests it directly.

AI density measured exactly zero because no repository in the corpus records
machine authorship. That is the signal behaving as designed, not a null result.

## Run 4 · the differentiator, measured directly

Precision@10 is the wrong instrument for hidden coupling. A signal firing on
seventy file pairs across a repository can be entirely correct and still move a
top-ten list by a fraction of a point, so a −0.8 pp ablation delta is weak
evidence either way. `CheckCoupling` asks the claim as the spec states it:

> if touching A and forgetting B is what breaks newcomers, and the signal finds
> those pairs first, that is what makes the tool feel clairvoyant

Stated as something falsifiable: a newcomer's corrective commits should land on
hidden-coupled files more often than that same newcomer's ordinary commits do.
Lift is those two rates divided. 1.0 is no relationship.

This machinery had existed since the backtest package was written and was never
reachable from the command line, so the product's central claim had never been
measured. It is now `lectio backtest --coupling`.

```
  repository                 pairs   fixes on coupled base rate    lift
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  hashicorp__hcl                16      87         7        4%    1.90
  spf13__afero                   6      43        16       28%    1.31
  charmbracelet__bubbletea     189      99        59       46%    1.30
  charmbracelet__lipgloss       54      52        43       68%    1.21
  ...
  go-git__go-git               235     275       127       52%    0.89
  uber-go__zap                  53      46        17       47%    0.78
  julienschmidt__httprouter      1      15         5       64%    0.52
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  pooled                      1554    2311      1515       64%    1.02
```

**Lift 1.02, over 2,311 newcomer corrective commits and 1,554 coupled pairs
across 29 repositories.** Twenty-four repositories cleared both sample-size
guards; their lifts cluster tightly around 1.0, and the two largest excursions
sit on the thinnest evidence — hcl's 1.90 rests on a 4% base rate, where the
ratio is unstable by construction.

One limitation stood here for four runs and has since been fixed. `relatedness`
uses call edges to decide whether a co-change is already explained by a static
dependency, so a revision whose dependencies resolve badly reports *more*
hidden pairs than one that resolves cleanly — the signal is inflated by its own
analysis failing. go-git gave 235 pairs on one run and 233 on another. The
check now applies the same `MaxDegraded` guard Gate A does and skips a
repository whose packages fail to type-check, so the numbers above are the last
ones produced without it. A 1% effect on a ratio that came out at 1.02 changes
nothing; on a ratio that came out above 1 it would have.

This is not a sample-size problem. It is the clearest result in the four runs:
**hidden coupling does not predict where newcomers go wrong.** The signal finds
real co-change pairs that no import explains — 1,554 of them — and corrective
work does not concentrate on them.

The product is staked on this signal. Centrality and churn are computable by
anyone; the spec's case for the tool existing is that co-change minus static
dependency surfaces what a senior engineer knows and cannot articulate. On this
corpus it surfaces something, and that something has no relationship to where
newcomers get into trouble.

## Runs 5 and 6 · symbol granularity, and the pass that is not one

Every measure in runs 1 through 3 scored a set of file paths, and file size is
a strong prior on any of them. That was the last explanation left standing:
maybe the ranking is fine and the *unit* was wrong. Lectio ranks symbols
natively and only collapsed to files because the harness required it, so the
collapse was throwing away the thing being measured.

Grading declarations needed three pieces: line ranges per commit, a syntax-only
parse of each changed file at the revision where the change landed, and a match
back to the symbol table **by name**. Names rather than positions, because by
the time a newcomer commits the file has moved under them — the regression test
puts `Parse` at lines 3–5 in the index and 103–105 at commit time, which any
position-based match gets wrong.

The baselines had to be restated, and **that restatement was fixed before any
symbol-level number existed**, because choosing it afterwards is how a gate gets
argued past. Each keeps its own signal and inherits it: "the biggest files"
becomes "the symbols in the biggest files, in source order" — what the
file-level baseline actually recommended — not "the biggest symbols".

```
  strategy                      prec@10     recall        MRR   median
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  lectio                          10.5%       5.6%      0.268    10.0%
  most churned, 12mo               8.3%       4.5%      0.178     0.0%
  largest files                    6.9%       3.8%      0.163     0.0%
  most distinct authors            6.5%       3.6%      0.159     0.0%
  size-proportional draw           6.0%       4.2%      0.164     0.0%
  most recently modified           4.7%       1.9%      0.139     0.0%
  largest symbols                 22.8%      13.8%      0.467    20.0%

PASS beat all 4 baselines — but largest symbols scored higher, and it is a control
```

**Lectio passes the gate as written, and the pass means nothing.** The
largest-symbols control more than doubles it, and leads on every column: recall
13.8% against 5.6%, MRR 0.467 against 0.268, median 20% against 10%.

That control exists precisely to ask this question, and it was defined and
committed before the run. It asks whether size simply follows the measure down
from files to symbols. It does. Changing granularity did not remove the size
prior, it relocated it — and against a size heuristic expressed at the matching
granularity, the ranking loses by more than it ever lost at file level.

Banding by declaration size does not rescue it either:

```
  strategy                   Q1 smallest          Q2          Q3  Q4 largest
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  lectio                           11.0%        7.5%        8.3%       13.7%
  largest symbols                  10.3%       14.1%       11.9%       23.7%
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  size spread within band              3x          2x          3x         23x
```

Lectio takes the smallest band by 0.7 points and loses the other three by 6.6,
3.6 and 10.0. Q1 through Q3 are tight — 3×, 2×, 3× — so this is not a
composition artifact the way the file-level Q1 result was; where size is
genuinely held constant, largest-symbols is still ahead.

That result forced a change to the reading rule, which is worth stating
plainly because this document argues elsewhere that rules should be fixed in
advance. The rule counted bands and ignored margins, so it called one narrow
win against three heavy losses "mixed". It now weighs the mean per-band margin
against the ablation's existing noise floor. The change is symmetric — it makes
a narrow *lectio* win read as unsettled by exactly the same threshold, and a
test asserts that — so it is a correction to a rule that misreported rather
than a threshold moved to obtain an answer.

Run 6 repeats the pattern on corrective commits, with a wider baseline margin
and the same ceiling: lectio 7.7%, best baseline 1.9%, largest symbols 17.1%.
Thin at 31 cases — 62 of 114 have too few corrected declarations to score — so
read it as agreeing with run 5 rather than as independent evidence.

A control cannot fail the gate. The spec names four baselines and adding a
fifth after seeing the score is the goalpost move the baseline/control split
exists to prevent, so the verdict stays PASS. It is now printed in warning
colour with what undercuts it on the same line, because a PASS beside a control
that doubled it is the most misleading thing this report could print.

## Run 7 · size removed by construction

Stratification narrows the size range inside a band; it does not eliminate it.
Q1 spanned 23× at file level and 3× at declaration level, so every earlier
conclusion carried the caveat that a size strategy still held real size
information inside the band it was being judged in.

Matched pairs remove the axis entirely. Each ground-truth declaration is paired
with one of the *same size* the contributor did not touch, and the question is
which of the two a strategy ranks higher. Within a pair the candidates are the
same size, so a strategy ranking purely by size wins exactly half. **Chance is
50%, which makes the number readable without any baseline beside it** — the
only measure in this document with that property.

```
  strategy                      prec@10   matched
  ------------------------------------------------
  largest symbols                 22.8%     52.0%
  lectio                          10.5%     49.5%
  lectio −centrality               8.1%     51.3%
  lectio −hidden_coupling         10.7%     48.1%
  most churned, 12mo               8.3%     49.9%
  largest files                    6.9%     48.4%
  size-proportional draw           6.0%     51.2%
  most distinct authors            6.5%     48.3%
  most recently modified           4.7%     49.2%

  2,891 pairs from 67 cases
```

Every strategy lands between 48.1% and 52.0%. The spread is smaller than the
±2 points this corpus can resolve at all.

> The ±2 was chosen by eye, and at 2,891 pairs it happens to be about right —
> a 95% interval over that many pairs is ±1.8. It is *not* right at file
> granularity, where the same figure was applied to 426 and 577 pairs and
> should have been nearer ±5. Runs 9 and 10 compute it instead.

**The control makes the point cleanly.** Largest-symbols scored 22.8% on
precision — more than double lectio, the result that undercut run 5's pass —
and 52.0% here. Its entire advantage was size. Presented with two declarations
of equal length it cannot tell which one a newcomer touched, because length was
all it ever knew.

**And neither can the ranking.** Lectio scores 49.5%. Seven signals, a call
graph, a PageRank, twelve months of history, and the result is a coin. Every
ablation variant is inside the same band, which is what you would expect if
none of the signals were contributing anything the pairing had not already
removed.

### The same instrument at file granularity

Files have sizes too, and the file targets are the spec's primary measure, so
the pairing applies there as well:

```
  strategy                      prec@10   matched
  ------------------------------------------------
  most churned, 12mo               40.0%     56.5%
  most distinct authors            39.8%     53.3%
  most recently modified           39.3%     52.6%
  size-proportional draw           44.1%     51.6%
  lectio                           41.4%     50.5%
  largest files                    47.6%     49.1%

  426 pairs from 33 cases, case set 499d03b5ecd3
```

**Largest-files, which won every file-granularity run, scores 49.1%.** Same
result as its symbol-level counterpart, same reason: shown two files of equal
size it cannot tell which one a newcomer touched.

Lectio scores 50.5% — chance again.

**Churn at 56.5% is the one cell worth following up.** It is the only number
across both granularities clear of the ±2 point margin — a margin that was
half what it should have been here, as run 9 later computed — and it is a
signal
*inside* lectio: removing churn drops the ranking to 49.5%, and the three
history-derived baselines are the three highest rows. That is consistent with
churn carrying a little information beyond size while the other six signals
dilute it back to a coin.

#### It replicates

Widening to twelve cases per repository — 138 scored, 577 pairs from 47 —
gives the same answer:

| Strategy | 5 cases/repo | 12 cases/repo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| most churned, 12mo | 56.5% | **55.8%** |
| most distinct authors | 53.3% | 53.7% |
| most recently modified | 52.6% | 53.5% |
| size-proportional draw | 51.6% | 51.6% |
| lectio | 50.5% | 52.2% |
| largest files | 49.1% | 49.9% |

**The pattern is coherent, which matters more than any single cell.** All three
history-derived baselines sit above chance in both runs; both size-derived
strategies sit on it. That is exactly the split the measure was built to
expose, and it is not what twelve independent noise draws look like.

So the honest reading is no longer "one cell worth following". Something in
twelve months of commit history predicts which of two equally-sized files a

it is real enough to survive a doubling of the sample.

everything else here: churn carries a real, modest signal; orphaning actively
cancels it; the remaining four are indistinguishable from nothing on a measure
size cannot win.

It is also invisible on precision, where orphaning scores −0.4 pp and reads as
noise. The two columns disagree because precision rewards preferring larger

now reports both.

Two caveats. These deltas are computed from the strategy table rather than

pairs from 47 cases makes a 3-point delta suggestive rather than settled.

> **Superseded.** This result did not survive either test that was later run
> against it: −0.1 points on the holdout (run 8), and +1.9 with overlapping
> intervals when gate-a itself was re-run with error bars (run 9). The
> subsection is left as written because the reasoning that follows from it is
> the reasoning a three-point delta invites, and that is the point. The number
> was right; the margin it was read against was half the width it should have
> been.

### Why this number can be trusted

The first version of this measure produced 28.8% to 46.8% — every strategy
below chance, which is impossible on a size-matched pair. The pairing was at
fault: candidates are sorted by (size, ID) and ground truth is processed in ID
order, so twins were consumed in ascending ID order and almost always carried a
lower ID than their partner. Every strategy here breaks ties by ID, so all of
them inherited that ordering at once.

That failure is the reason the measure now carries four calibrations, and they
should be read as part of the result rather than as test hygiene:

| Calibration | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| A size-only ranking | must score chance |
| The same ranking reversed | must score chance |
| Plain ID order | must score chance |
| An oracle that knows the answer | must score ~100% |

The first three catch a leak; the fourth catches a measure with no power to
detect anything. On the synthetic corpus a size-only ranking scored 3.3% under
the old pairing and 50.0% under the new one.

does not transfer: precision averages over ten predictions per case while a
matched-pair accuracy averages over as few as five, and the file-level pairing
reaches only about half the cases, so which cases survive matters far more
here. Any matched-pair figure at file granularity should be read as ±3 before
its interval is even consulted.

Churn's interval is 50.4 – 61.1, which does not reach 50. Every other row's

with the margin computed rather than assumed — and the margin is roughly ±5
points, not the ±2 that was written down.

Churn's sign test is **9 of 14 repositories, p = 0.42**. A coin.

The two readings disagree, and the disagreement is informative rather than a
contradiction. The bootstrap estimates the mean over cases, so a repository
that produced nine cases weighs nine times one that produced one. The sign test
gives every repository one vote. Churn clearing chance on the first and not the
second means its advantage is concentrated in the repositories that happened to
produce the most cases — which is exactly the pattern the sign test was added
to make visible, and exactly what a corpus average cannot show.

When the two disagree, the sign test is the conservative reading and the one to
take. **Nothing at file granularity on this corpus has been shown to beat
chance repository by repository.**

not the +3.0 the earlier run reported, and the two intervals — 49.3 – 59.7 and

returned as −0.1, is inside the noise on the corpus that produced it. It was
never a three-point effect. It was a three-point *sample*, on 577 pairs, read
against a margin that had been chosen by eye and was less than half the width
it should have been.

That is the whole case for computing the error bar from the run. The holdout
caught this one because it was cheap to fetch thirty more repositories. The
interval would have caught it without a second corpus at all.

Every leave-one-out variant lands between 52.0% and 55.3%, and every one of
their intervals contains lectio's point estimate as well as chance. Read as
deltas the ablation still says centrality and churn are the two signals worth
having — removing either costs about 1.4 points, more than removing any other.
Read as intervals it says nothing at all, which is the honest summary of a
seven-signal ablation on 707 pairs from 17 repositories.

### What run 9 does not overturn

The direction of the history/size split survives: the three history baselines
occupy 54.0 – 55.9% and the two size strategies 48.7 – 52.1%, with no
history strategy below any size strategy. It is the *magnitude* and the
*ordering within each group* that the intervals dissolve.

And the case set moved again — 161 cases here against 138 on the run this one
repeats, 707 pairs against 577. Which cases survive depends on whether each
rewound revision's dependencies resolve, and after nine runs that is no longer
a footnote about reproducibility. At file granularity the matched measure
reaches roughly a third of the attempted cases, so the population it lands on
varies more than any effect it has found.

### The 1.25x anomaly was leakage, and the sweep shows it leaking

Largest-files walks steadily upward as the bound loosens: **50.5%, 52.9%,

accuracy, exactly as it should, and it is the cleanest possible demonstration
that the pairing bound is doing real work.

At **1.10x — the tightest ratio with usable coverage — largest-files scores
50.5%**, dead on chance and not clear of it. Run 8's 52.9% was the bound
letting size back in. The question that section left open is answered.

It also means every matched-pair number in this document is slightly generous
to whatever correlates with size, 1.25x included, and the correction is about
two points at file granularity.

### The history/size split is sharper at 1.10x than at 1.25x

At the tight end, four strategies clear chance and all four are history-derived:
recency 58.6%, authorship 57.8%, churn 57.3%, churn-only 57.3%. Both
size-derived strategies sit on chance — largest-files at 50.5%, the
size-proportional draw at 48.5%. Lectio is at 51.8% and does not clear it.

That is the split run 8 reported, measured at a bound that leaks less, and it
comes out cleaner rather than weaker. It is the one finding in this document
that has now survived a second corpus and a change of instrument.

### The loose end is where everything becomes significant, which is the warning

At 2.00x nine of ten strategies clear chance, including largest-files at 58.7%
and lectio at 56.3%. Nothing was learned between 1.25x and 2.00x except that a
two-to-one size difference is easy to detect. A result that appears only at the
loose end of this ladder is a result about the bound, and the sweep exists so
that is visible rather than arguable.

### The tight end broke the interval, and that was a bug in this repository

The 1.00x column reached four repositories, and largest-files scored 60.3%
there with an interval that cleared chance. On exact size matches a size
ranking cannot know anything — it is the same impossibility that exposed the
original pairing bug.

This time the pairing was sound and the interval was not. A percentile
bootstrap over four clusters draws from 35 distinct resamples; the interval it
produces is arbitrary rather than merely wide, and it can come out narrow.
`BootstrapInterval` now refuses below eight repositories and reports the point
estimate with no interval, which is what the 1.00x column should have said all
along. The column stays in the table, unbolded, as the thing it is: 171 pairs
from four repositories, and not evidence.

### The bound does almost nothing here, and that is the finding

At file granularity largest-files walked 50.5% → 58.7% across the ladder. Here
largest-symbols walks 51.1% → 53.0%: the same direction, a tenth of the
distance. Every other row moves by less than a point and coverage barely
changes — tightening from 2.00x to 1.00x costs 18% of the pairs, where at file
granularity it cost 81% of them.

The reason is that declarations are dense in size space and files are not. A
repository has thousands of declarations, so for almost any one of them there
is another within a percent or two of the same length; the twin found at 2.00x
was already an exact match, and tightening the bound removes almost nothing. A
repository has tens of files, so the nearest file by size can be half again as
large, and there the bound is doing all the work.

This is why the two granularities' results should not be read with the same
confidence. **The file-level numbers are sensitive to a constant nobody
derived. The declaration-level numbers are not.**

### Which makes run 7 the strongest result in this document

The exact-match column is the cleanest measurement the project has produced:
2,496 pairs from 60 cases across 24 repositories, every pair between two
declarations of *identical* length, so there is no residual size for any
strategy to convert into accuracy. Every strategy lands between 47.6% and
51.4%, and none of them clears chance.

Seven signals, a call graph, a PageRank, twelve months of history, four
baselines and a size control, and among declarations of exactly the same size
none of them can tell which one a newcomer touched.

### The case set reproduced, and that revises a caveat

This run scored 75 cases with fingerprint `0d1e81666c55` — the same case set as
run 5 and run 7, weeks earlier and from a different binary.

The document has said since run 1 that the case set is unstable. That is true
of the **file** targets, which have produced a different fingerprint on nearly
every repeat: 85 then 77, 138 then 161. It is not true of the **declaration**
targets, which have now produced `0d1e81666c55` three times.

The holdout points the same way: its first run gave `09f9c537b9ac` and its next
two, hours apart and across a binary change, both gave `72749af8fce5`.

So drift is not a property of the harness in general. It is concentrated in
which cases the file targets admit, and it moves with the state of the module
cache rather than with anything in the run. Two consequences:

- Quoting a file-granularity total across runs needs the fingerprint checked
  first, and `lectio compare` now rebuilds over the shared cases when it does
  not match.
- Quoting a declaration-granularity total does not. Three runs, one
  fingerprint.

### It is a strict superset, and now that is checkable

The write-up said of the equivalent file-level pair, four runs ago:

> The second run *contains* the first — more cases per repository, same
> repositories — so it is a larger sample rather than an independent one.

That was an assumption. `lectio compare` now confirms it and reports nothing
else:

```
One run's cases are entirely contained in the other's: every case the
first run scored, the second run scored too. Nothing was measured in one
and not the other, so this compares sample sizes rather than populations.

  strategy                   prec before      after         Δ Δ matched
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  largest symbols                 22.8%      22.8%     +0.0     +0.0
```

Zero cases dropped from the narrower run, 67 from the wider, and **every shared

and they are the reason this comparison was worth running at all:

- Case selection is deterministic, and `--cases 12` strictly extends

- A case's score does not depend on what else was in the run.
- The intersection machinery is correct: restricting the 142-case run to the 75

A "different case set" is therefore not always a different population, and
`lectio compare` distinguishes the two rather than issuing the same warning for
both.

## Run 13 · the same data, re-read

Run 10's report was replayed after the coverage split, the sign test and the
leak check landed — same case set `72749af8fce5`, no corpus touched. That is
what `--replay` is for: the analysis has changed more often than the data, and
every column added this month had otherwise cost another pass over a corpus.

**The binding limit is the floor, not the bound.** The pairing reached 41 of 74
cases and **864 of 1,606 touched items (54%)**. Of the 33 cases it did not
score, **28 built pairs and fell below the floor of five** and only 5 found no
size-matched twin at all. That is the corpus being thin, not the bound being
tight — and the sweep agrees: loosening from 1.25x to 2.00x buys six cases
(41 → 47) at the cost of eight points of leakage on a size control.

This corrects the obvious-sounding fix. More cases per repository would help;
a looser ratio would not, and would cost more than it bought.

Churn-only at p = 0.035 is the first strategy in thirteen runs to come in under

So the honest statement is narrow and worth making anyway: **churn-only is the
only strategy that has ever cleared this test, on either corpus, and it clears
it by an amount that testing ten strategies explains.** It is not evidence. It
is the one place a next experiment would be pointed.

### The sign-test result did not replicate

Churn-only wins **9 of 15 repositories on gate-a, p = 0.607.** On the holdout
it was 12 of 15 at p = 0.035. That is not a weakened effect, it is a coin —
and after Holm correction across the ten strategies each table scores, both

So the one lead run 13 produced is closed the way the orphaning result was

That is now two pre-registered candidate findings that failed to replicate and
zero that held. The pattern is worth naming: **every effect this project has
found at file granularity has been the largest number in a table, and none has
survived being looked for somewhere else.**

### The floor binds here too

Coverage: 60 of 161 cases and **950 of 1,785 touched items (53%)**. Of the 101
cases the pairing did not score, **90 fell below the floor of five** and 11
found no twin at all — the same shape as the holdout, at nearly three times the
scale. Twelve cases per repository buys cases, not repositories: 17 of them
produced pairs here, exactly as at five per repository.

**That is the ceiling on the file-level measure, and it is structural.** More
cases per repository add pairs to repositories already counted; the bootstrap
and the sign test both resample repositories, and a corpus of thirty yields
seventeen with pairs however hard it is run. A file-granularity result that
needs more than seventeen repositories cannot be had from this corpus at any
setting.

### Seventeen repositories is the ceiling, and raising --cases does not move it

Both the bootstrap interval and the sign test resample repositories. At file
granularity the corpus yields seventeen with usable pairs at five cases per
repository, and seventeen at twelve — the extra cases land in repositories
already counted. Coverage now reports the repository count for that reason.

So a file-level result needing more than seventeen repositories cannot be had
from this corpus at any setting, and the intervals it produces stay near ±5
points however hard the harness is run. The declaration targets reach
twenty-five, which is most of why they are the ones to quote.

## What this means

By the spec's own rule, **phase 1 has failed** — not on a technicality, and not
because of how it was measured. Fourteen attempts to find a reading where the
ranking earns its place came back negative, across two disjoint corpora,
including the one the spec proposed as the tiebreaker and the one that removed
the file-path prior altogether.

Four findings, and the last two are the serious ones.

**The ranking adds nothing over file size.** Once size is held constant, lectio's
ordering is indistinguishable from sorting by file length — 0.2 and 0.3 points
apart in the two bands where size is genuinely controlled. It is not choosing
badly. It is not choosing differently. A random draw weighted by file size
outscores it.

**The differentiator does not differentiate.** Hidden coupling is what the spec
offers as the reason this tool should exist rather than a shell alias. Measured
directly, on twenty-eight times the sample the gate itself uses, it has no
relationship to where newcomers go wrong. That is not a tuning problem, and no
weighting fixes a signal whose lift is 1.02.

**Size is not an artifact of the unit.** Grading declarations instead of file
paths was the last standing explanation under which the ranking might be better
than it measured. It is now tested, and the answer is that size followed the
measure down: the longest declarations predict newcomer work more than twice as
well as seven signals do. The prior was never about files.

**Nothing found here has replicated except a direction.** Three candidate
findings were named, tested on the corpus that did not produce them, and all
three came back a coin: removing orphaning (+3.0 pp → −0.1), churn-only winning
most repositories (p = 0.035 → 0.607), and four strategies clearing chance at
the tightest usable bound (four → none). Each was the largest number in a table
of ten at the time it was quoted. What survives is that history-derived
strategies rank above size-derived ones, on both corpora and at every pairing
bound — a direction with no magnitude attached to it.

**And with size removed by construction, nothing predicts anything — at
declaration granularity.** Run 7 is the cleanest result here: every strategy
inside ±2 points of chance over 2,891 size-matched pairs, including the size
control whose precision advantage evaporates the moment its two candidates are
the same length. That is not a ranking that is behind — it is a question that
git history, at that granularity, does not appear to answer.

**At file granularity something is there, and it is not the ranking.** The
history-derived baselines clear chance on both corpora, by four to nine points,
and the size-derived ones do not. Run 8 is the only test in this document that
was run against repositories that had produced none of the hypotheses being
tested, and it is the only place a claim here has independent support. What it
supports is a one-line heuristic — sort by last-modified — that beats seven
signals, a call graph and a PageRank by 3.9 points.

What would change the answer, in rough order of cost:

0. ~~**Test a two-signal ranking on an independent corpus.**~~ Done — this was
   run 8, and runs 8 and 9 together dissolved most of what it was built to
   confirm. Churn-only is +1.8 over lectio on the holdout and behind three of
   the four baselines; the orphaning result that motivated the exercise did not
   replicate; and with error bars the ordering inside the history group is not
   resolvable at all.

   What survives is one direction — history above size — and one row whose
   interval clears chance on one corpus while its repositories do not agree
   with it. That is not a two-signal ranking. It is a reason to stop looking
   for one here.

0b. ~~**Get more repositories into the matched measure.**~~ Closed by run 14,
   and the answer is that this corpus cannot. Seventeen repositories produce
   usable pairs at five cases each and seventeen at twelve — extra cases land
   in repositories already counted, and both the interval and the sign test
   resample repositories. Every file-level interval here is near ±5 points
   because of that, and no candidate ranking can be separated from another at
   that width.

   Neither lever moves it. Loosening the bound buys six cases and eight points
   of leakage on a size control; raising `--cases` buys pairs and no power. Of
   the cases the pairing misses, 90 of 101 fell below the floor of five rather
   than failing to find a twin — the corpus is thin at the level of individual
   contributors, not tight at the level of size matching.

   So the file target is the wrong instrument, and the declaration target —
   115 of 142 cases, 3,823 pairs, 25 repositories — is the one to quote. A
   larger corpus would be the only thing that changed the file-level answer,
   at linear cost and square-root benefit.

0c. **Stop believing the largest number in a table.** Three candidate findings
   have now been named, quoted, and killed by the other corpus, and every one
   of them was the smallest p or the biggest delta in a table of ten at the
   moment it was believed. The harness now corrects for that — Holm across the
   strategies a report scores, with the nominal-only row marked rather than
   promoted — but the discipline is the point and the code is only a reminder.

   The rule that would have caught all three: a number is a finding when it
   survives a corpus that did not produce it. Nothing else in this document
   has ever been one.

1. **A different definition of hidden coupling.** The current one is Jaccard
   co-change minus static reachability. Run 4 falsifies that specific
   definition, not the intuition behind it. Any replacement should be proposed
   before seeing which one scores best, and tested with `--coupling` rather
   than through the gate.
2. ~~**A size-orthogonal target.**~~ Built and run — this was run 7. It asks
   exactly the intended question, calibrates correctly, and returns chance for
   every strategy. It remains the right instrument for testing any *future*
   signal, since a candidate that cannot beat chance on it is not carrying
   information beyond size.
3. **A different premise.** The backtest measures what people touched, which was
   always a stand-in for what they needed to understand. Those may simply not
   be close enough, in which case Gate A is unanswerable from git history and
   the question needs users rather than a corpus.

   After fourteen runs this is no longer one reading among several. The
   size-matched result says that among declarations of equal size, *nothing*
   available here — not centrality, not churn, not co-change, not recency, not
   authorship — separates the ones a newcomer touched from the ones they did
   not. Either the signals are all uninformative, or what a newcomer touches is
   not a function of the properties git can see. The second is more likely, and
   it is a question about people rather than about repositories.

   The file-granularity results do not rescue this. They point the same way
   once their error is computed and their multiplicity corrected: across
   fourteen runs and two corpora, every row that ever cleared a threshold
   cleared it on one corpus and not the other.

What should not happen: tuning weights against these numbers. Every strategy
that beat lectio beat it by being more size-correlated, so fitting to this
metric produces a tool that recommends the biggest thing available — the
failure mode the product exists to prevent. The weights are unchanged from
before all ten runs, and the orphaning episode is why: a three-point ablation
delta that was worth acting on for weeks turned out to be +1.9 with overlapping
intervals on its own corpus and −0.1 on another. Anything fitted to it would
have been fitted to noise.
